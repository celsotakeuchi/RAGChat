# EU Air Passenger Rights: Public Knowledge Base

- **Audience:** public
- **Language:** English
- **Version:** 1.0
- **Prepared:** 27 October 2026, from the text of Regulation (EC) No 261/2004 and secondary summaries of the case law and of the 2026 reform. Verify against EUR-Lex before relying on it.
- **Disclaimer:** This is a plain-language summary written for a technical demonstration. It is not legal advice and is not affiliated with any airline, authority or claims company.

## AIR-01 What the rules cover

Regulation (EC) No 261/2004 sets common rules on compensation and assistance for air passengers in three situations: denied boarding, cancellation and long delay.

It applies to passengers who:
- depart from an airport in the European Union, on any airline; or
- depart from an airport outside the EU and fly to an airport in the EU on an EU airline, unless they already received benefits or compensation and assistance in that third country.

Through agreements, the rules also extend to some non-EU European countries. The rules do not generally apply to passengers travelling free of charge or at a reduced fare that is not available to the public.

## AIR-02 Conditions to claim

To be entitled to compensation, a passenger normally needs:
- a confirmed reservation on the flight; and
- to be present for check-in at the time stated by the airline, tour operator or travel agent, or, if no time was stated, at least 45 minutes before the scheduled departure.

For delays, the time that counts for compensation is the arrival at the final destination. Case law treats the arrival time as the moment at least one door of the aircraft opens. If a connecting flight is missed on a single booking, the delay is measured at the final destination.

## AIR-03 Denied boarding

Denied boarding means the airline refuses to carry a passenger who has a confirmed reservation and has presented in time, usually because the flight is overbooked.

- The airline must first ask for volunteers. Volunteers receive benefits agreed with the airline, plus the choice described in AIR-10 and the care described in AIR-11.
- If there are not enough volunteers, passengers may be denied boarding against their will. They are then entitled to compensation (see AIR-08), the choice in AIR-10 and care (AIR-11).
- Boarding can be refused for reasonable grounds, such as health, safety, security or inadequate travel documents. In that case the compensation rules do not apply.

## AIR-04 Cancellation: passenger rights

When a flight is cancelled, the passenger is entitled to:
- the choice between a refund, a return flight and rerouting (AIR-10);
- care while waiting (AIR-11);
- compensation (AIR-08), unless one of the exceptions in AIR-05 or AIR-12 applies.

## AIR-05 Cancellation: when there is no compensation

A cancelled flight does not give a right to compensation if:
- the passenger was informed at least two weeks before the scheduled departure; or
- the passenger was informed between two weeks and seven days before, and was offered rerouting that leaves no more than two hours earlier and arrives less than four hours after the original arrival time; or
- the passenger was informed less than seven days before, and was offered rerouting that leaves no more than one hour earlier and arrives less than two hours after the original arrival time; or
- the cancellation was caused by extraordinary circumstances (AIR-12).

The airline must prove that one of these exceptions applies.

## AIR-06 Long delays: when care and reimbursement apply

The following departure delays trigger the right to care (AIR-11):
- 2 hours or more for flights of 1,500 km or less;
- 3 hours or more for flights within the EU of more than 1,500 km, and for all other flights between 1,500 and 3,500 km;
- 4 hours or more for all other flights.

If the delay is at least 5 hours, the passenger may also choose to give up the journey and receive a refund of the ticket (AIR-10). Where relevant, the airline must also provide a return flight to the first point of departure.

## AIR-07 Long delays: compensation after 3 hours

The text of the Regulation does not state a right to compensation for delays. This right comes from case law of the Court of Justice of the European Union: a passenger whose flight arrives at the final destination three hours or more late is treated like a passenger whose flight was cancelled, and may claim the same compensation (AIR-08).

Compensation does not apply if the delay was caused by extraordinary circumstances (AIR-12). The 3-hour rule applies to arrival delay, while the care thresholds in AIR-06 apply to departure delay.

## AIR-08 Compensation amounts

For denied boarding, qualifying cancellations and delays of three hours or more at arrival, compensation per passenger is:
- **EUR 250** for flights of 1,500 km or less;
- **EUR 400** for flights within the EU of more than 1,500 km, and for all other flights between 1,500 and 3,500 km;
- **EUR 600** for all other flights.

The distance is measured as the direct (great-circle) distance from the first departure airport to the final destination. Compensation is paid in cash, by electronic bank transfer, bank order or bank cheque. It can be paid in travel vouchers only if the passenger agrees in writing.

## AIR-09 Reduction by half after rerouting

If the airline offers rerouting to the final destination and the new arrival time is not later than the following limits after the original arrival time, the compensation may be reduced by 50 percent:
- 2 hours for flights of 1,500 km or less;
- 3 hours for flights within the EU of more than 1,500 km, and for all other flights between 1,500 and 3,500 km;
- 4 hours for all other flights.

## AIR-10 Refund, return flight or rerouting

When a passenger is denied boarding, a flight is cancelled, or a delay reaches 5 hours, the passenger may choose between:
1. a refund of the ticket within seven days for the part of the journey not made (and for parts already made if the flight no longer serves the passenger's purpose), together with a return flight to the first point of departure at the earliest opportunity where relevant;
2. rerouting to the final destination at the earliest opportunity, under comparable transport conditions; or
3. rerouting at a later date of the passenger's choice, subject to seat availability.

## AIR-11 Care: meals, hotel and communication

While waiting, the airline must offer free of charge:
- meals and refreshments in reasonable relation to the waiting time;
- hotel accommodation, if one or more nights are needed, and transport between the airport and the accommodation;
- two telephone calls, or messages by email, telex or fax.

Passengers with reduced mobility, their companions, certified service animals and unaccompanied children must be given priority. The duty to provide care applies even when the delay or cancellation was caused by extraordinary circumstances.

## AIR-12 Extraordinary circumstances

An airline does not have to pay compensation if it proves that the disruption was caused by extraordinary circumstances that could not have been avoided even if all reasonable measures had been taken.

Examples that may qualify, depending on the facts, include political instability, weather conditions that make the flight impossible, security risks, unexpected flight safety shortcomings and strikes that disrupt operations. Each case is judged on its own facts, and the airline carries the burden of proof.

Case law has held that technical problems that are part of the normal operation of an aircraft are generally not extraordinary circumstances. The exemption removes the duty to compensate, but not the duty to provide care (AIR-11).

## AIR-13 Downgrading

If an airline places a passenger in a lower class than the one paid for, the passenger is entitled to a refund, within seven days, of a share of the ticket price for the affected leg:
- 30 percent for flights of 1,500 km or less;
- 50 percent for flights within the EU of more than 1,500 km (with an exception for the French overseas departments), and for all other flights between 1,500 and 3,500 km;
- 75 percent for all other flights.

If a passenger is placed in a higher class, the airline cannot ask for additional payment.

## AIR-14 Further compensation and recourse

The Regulation does not stop a passenger from claiming further compensation under other rules, for example national law or the Montreal Convention. Compensation already paid under the Regulation may be deducted from that further compensation.

The airline's obligations cannot be limited or waived by contract terms or notices. An airline that pays can seek reimbursement from third parties, such as a tour operator.

## AIR-15 Information and complaints

Airlines must display a notice at check-in with the rules on compensation and assistance. They must give a written notice to passengers affected by denied boarding or cancellation, and to those delayed by at least two hours.

A passenger who is not satisfied should first complain to the airline. If the problem is not solved, the passenger may complain to the national enforcement body of the country where the flight departed (or, for flights arriving from outside the EU, of the country of arrival). In Spain this body is the Spanish Aviation Safety Agency (AESA). The passenger may also use an out-of-court dispute procedure or go to court. Time limits for claims depend on national law.

## AIR-16 Upcoming changes (NOT CURRENT LAW)

**This section describes future rules. Do not apply them to current flights.**

According to published summaries, a revision of the Regulation was adopted as Regulation (EU) 2026/2202 of 16 September 2026, amending Regulation (EC) No 261/2004 and Regulation (EC) No 2027/97. Its new rules do not yet apply. Until the amending rules enter into application, the current rules and the case law described in this knowledge base govern every claim.

The reform was reported to keep the 3-hour delay threshold and the compensation amounts of EUR 250, 400 and 600. It was reported to add or clarify rules on extraordinary circumstances, complaint procedures, price transparency, hand luggage and the rights of passengers with reduced mobility. The date from which these rules apply was reported to be in the second half of 2027 at the earliest, but this should be checked in the Official Journal.

## AIR-17 What this knowledge base does not cover

This knowledge base does not answer questions about:
- lost, delayed or damaged baggage;
- package holidays and the duties of tour operators;
- the airline's liability for injury or damage under the Montreal Convention;
- detailed rights of passengers with reduced mobility;
- rail, bus, maritime and other passenger rights;
- exact deadlines for filing a claim, which depend on national law;
- the terms or procedures of any specific airline or claims company.

For these questions, consult the official sources or the airline directly.
